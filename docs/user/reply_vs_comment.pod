=head1 How to reply to or comment on a ticket

Request Tracker (RT) gives you two ways to add a message to a
L<ticket|docs/glossary.pod>, and the difference is who reads it. A
L<reply|docs/glossary.pod> is a message to the people the ticket is for: it is
emailed to the ticket's L<Requestors|docs/glossary.pod> and Ccs. A
L<comment|docs/glossary.pod> is an internal note: it is recorded on the ticket
but is not sent to the Requestors or Ccs. Reply when you are answering the
people you are helping; comment when you are leaving a note for your own team.

This guide describes a typical RT configuration; your RT may be set up
differently.

This page covers the two actions on their own. For where they fit in a ticket's
whole life, see L<Getting started with RT|docs/user/getting_started.pod>.

=head2 Reply or comment: which to use

The one thing to get right is who receives your message and any attachments you
add to it:

=over 4

=item *

A B<reply> goes out. RT emails it to the ticket's Requestors and Ccs, the
people the ticket is for. Use a reply to answer the requester, ask them a
question, or tell them what you have done. Often those people are outside your
organization, for example a customer who emailed your support address. They can
also be inside it, for example a colleague in another department who opened a
ticket with your team. Either way, a reply leaves RT as email, so write it as a
message the recipient will read. (RT also calls a reply I<correspondence>; you
will meet both words as you use RT, and the L<reply|docs/glossary.pod> glossary
entry notes the two are the same thing.)

=item *

A B<comment> stays in. RT records it on the ticket but does not send it to the
Requestors or Ccs. Use a comment for an internal working note, such as what you
found, what you are about to try, or a question for a teammate. Two cautions.
First, a comment is not private: RT keeps it on the ticket where anyone with
access can read it later, and it is emailed to the ticket's internal
L<watchers|docs/glossary.pod> (its AdminCcs). Do not put passwords, keys, or
other sensitive information in a comment. Second, mind your tone: write nothing
you would not want the requester, or a colleague, to see.

=back

Who exactly receives a reply or comment depends on the ticket's
L<roles|docs/glossary.pod>: its Requestors, Ccs, and AdminCcs. RT tracks these
roles on every ticket, so by default you do not have to work out who will see
your message yourself: RT already knows. The
L<reply|docs/glossary.pod> and L<comment|docs/glossary.pod> glossary entries
describe who is emailed in each case.

You can still adjust who is copied on any one message, though normally you
will not need to. The update screen always gives you one way to do that, and,
depending on your permissions and how your RT is set up, it can also preview
exactly who will be notified.

The controls you always have are B<One-time Cc> and B<One-time Bcc>. These two
fields, in the Message section, above the message box, copy someone on this one
message without adding them to the ticket's people. Put an address in either
field and only this update reaches them; the ticket's own Requestors, Ccs, and
AdminCcs are unchanged.

RT can also show you, before you submit, exactly who will receive this update.
Each recipient in the preview has a checkbox, so you can leave someone off this
one update without changing the ticket's people. To stop someone's notifications
for good, rather than just for this update, use the ticket's People page instead.
Whether you see a preview, and which form it takes, depends on your permissions
and your RT's settings: a default RT shows no preview at all, and your RT may
show one of two:

=over 4

=item *

The B<Scrips and Recipients> box is the detailed preview. It shows a
rule-by-rule breakdown of what RT will do when you submit: which
L<scrips|docs/glossary.pod> (RT's automated rules) will run, and the exact
addresses each one will email. Seeing this box takes a permission that many RTs
give to staff, so you may or may not have it. If you don't see it, your account
may not have that permission; ask your team lead.

=item *

The B<Recipients> list is the simpler preview. It shows a plain, flat list of
everyone who will receive mail, without the scrip-by-scrip detail. It appears
when your RT, or your own preferences, turns on the simplified recipient list.
When that setting is on, the B<Scrips and Recipients> box, for those who can see
it, starts collapsed at the bottom of the form.

=back

=head2 How to reply to a ticket

=over 4

=item 1

On the ticket's display page, open B<Actions> on the page menu, the menu for the
page you are on, and choose B<Reply>. RT opens the update screen with B<Update Type>
already set to B<Reply to requestors>. The message box is shaded red, a reminder that the
Requestor will receive what you type.

=item 2

Type your message in the box.

=item 3

Click B<Update Ticket>.

=back

RT returns to the display page, records your message in the ticket's history,
and emails it to the Requestors and Ccs. In a typical configuration, a
separate email is also sent to the Owner and AdminCcs.

=head2 How to comment on a ticket

=over 4

=item 1

On the ticket's display page, open B<Actions> on the page menu and choose
B<Comment>. RT opens the update screen with B<Update Type> already set
to B<Comments (Not sent to requestors)>. The message box is shaded yellow, a
reminder that your note stays internal.

=item 2

Type your note in the box.

=item 3

Click B<Update Ticket>.

=back

RT returns to the display page and records your note in the ticket's history. It
sends email to AdminCcs. It does not send email to the Requestors or Ccs.

=head2 Switch between reply and comment on the update screen

Reply and comment share one update screen. If you open the wrong one, or change
your mind, you do not have to start over: use the B<Update Type> control at the
top of the form to switch between B<Reply to requestors> and B<Comments (Not
sent to requestors)>. The message box changes color to match, light red for a reply
and yellow for a comment, and any recipient preview on the screen updates to
reflect the update you are about to send.

=head2 Change the ticket's status while you reply or comment

The same screen also has a B<Status> field. It defaults to the ticket's current
status, shown with B<(Unchanged)> after it, so an update you submit without
touching it leaves the status alone. Change it before you click B<Update Ticket>
to combine your reply or comment with a status change, for example replying to a
customer and resolving the ticket in one step.

=head2 Quote part of the history in your reply or comment

When you are answering a specific message, you can pull its text into your box so
your reply reads in context:

=over 4

=item *

B<Quote a whole entry.> Each message in the ticket's history has its own B<Reply>
and B<Comment> icons. Click one, and RT opens the update screen with that entry's
text already quoted in the box.

=item *

B<Quote just part of an entry.> Select the text you want inside a message in the
history, then click B<Reply> or B<Comment> (either the icon on that entry or
B<Actions> on the page menu). RT quotes only the text you selected. This is useful when an
entry has a lot of extra content, such as a long email signature, that you do
not need in your message.

=back

=head2 Reply or comment straight from search results

You do not have to open a ticket to reply to it or comment on it. RT's search
results can show a B<Reply> and a B<Comment> button for each ticket, letting
you answer straight from the list. Not every search shows these columns by
default. If yours does not, add them yourself: open B<Edit Search> on the page
menu, and under B<Display Columns>, add B<Reply> and B<Comment>. See the
L<Ticket Query Builder|docs/query_builder.pod> for more on customizing a
search's columns.

Click either button, and RT opens a popup right there on the list, without
leaving your search results. It is a shorter version of the update screen: a
B<Recipients> preview, with the same checkboxes to leave someone off this one
message, a Subject field, and the message box itself, shaded the same way as
the update screen, red for a reply and yellow for a comment. It does not
include the Status field, one-time Cc or Bcc, or attachments; open the ticket
itself and use B<Actions> on the page menu if you need any of those.

Click B<Send>, and RT records your message exactly as it would from the
ticket's own update screen: a new entry in the ticket's history, and the
right people notified. Your search results then refresh in place to show the
change, for example an updated "Last Updated" time, so you never have to
leave the list.

=head2 Reply or comment by email

You do not have to be in RT to reply or comment. RT sends replies and comments
as email, and answering that email is recorded exactly as if you had typed it
into the update screen: a new reply or comment in the ticket's history,
keeping the same reply-or-comment distinction.

RT then notifies the ticket's people exactly as it would for a web update: a
reply goes out to the Requestors and Ccs, and in a typical configuration a
separate copy to the Owner and AdminCcs; a comment goes only to the AdminCcs.
You do not have to build a To or Cc list, or use Reply All. RT already knows
the ticket's roles and takes care of notifying them, the same as if you had
answered from the update screen: type your reply, send it, and RT handles who
hears about it.

Each message also carries its own reply-to address, which routes an answer
back to the right place. A reply comes from the queue's correspondence
address; a comment comes from its comment address. When someone answers from
their mail client, their message goes to that address, so RT files it as the
same update type it was sent as:

=over 4

=item *

Answer a reply, and RT records your answer as a reply.

=item *

Answer a comment, and RT records your answer as a comment. (You need an email
in your inbox to respond to it, so usually only your team will comment via
email since they would have received a previous comment from the ticket.)

=back

This is why an email conversation on a ticket stays on the correct side of the
reply-or-comment line without anyone having to choose each time.

=head2 For administrators

Whether the update screen shows a recipient preview, and which kind, is
controlled by the C<ShowOutgoingEmail> right and the C<$SimplifiedRecipients>
configuration option.
