=head1 How to create a ticket in RT

This guide shows you how to create a L<ticket|docs/glossary.pod>: RT's record of
a single request or task, from arrival to completion. The recommended way is the
B<Create new ticket> button on the main menu, the navigation menu across the top of
every page, which opens a ticket-creation form. Other ways follow: by email, from the
homepage, from a person's user summary page, and automatically from another system.

This guide describes a typical RT configuration; your RT may be set up
differently.

If this is your first time in RT, the L<Getting started with RT|docs/user/getting_started.pod>
tutorial walks through creating a ticket, replying to it, and resolving it, all in
order. This page covers the creation task on its own, and in more depth.

=head2 Create a ticket from the main menu

=over 4

=item 1

On the main menu, click the B<Create new ticket> button. RT opens the
ticket-creation form in the default L<queue|docs/glossary.pod>.

On a narrow or mobile-width screen, this button shows less text: it condenses to
B<Create>, and to a B<+> at the narrowest widths.

=item 2

Check the B<Queue> shown in the B<Basics> box. If the ticket belongs in a different
queue, choose it from the dropdown.

=item 3

Set the people the ticket is for. In B<Requestors>, RT fills in your own address.
If the request is for someone else, remove your address and type the name or email
address of the person it is for, then pick them from the suggestions RT offers.

=item 4

In B<Subject>, write a short summary of the request. This is what people see in
ticket lists, so keep it specific. For what makes a subject useful, see
L</Write a useful subject line> below.

=item 5

In the message box, describe the request in full. This first message becomes the
start of the ticket's history.

=item 6

Fill in any other fields your queue requires, and leave the rest at their defaults.
Priority, dates, and other core fields are covered in L</Set priority, dates, and
other fields>; for the full set of fields you may see, see
L<Ticket metadata|docs/ticket_metadata.pod>.

=item 7

Click B<Create>. RT saves the ticket and shows its display page, with a
confirmation such as C<Ticket 42 created in queue 'General'> at the top. The number
is the ticket's permanent ID.

=back

Creating a ticket notifies the people involved by email: RT mails the
L<Requestor|docs/glossary.pod> an automatic confirmation. This is normal, and you can
turn it off for a single ticket with the B<Do not send email to Requestors on create>
checkbox under the B<Requestors> field.

=head2 Write a useful subject line

The subject is the first thing people see in ticket lists, in search results, and in
email about the ticket, so it is worth a moment's thought.

Include specific, unique information that clearly describes the request and makes the
ticket easy to find later. A subject like C<Laptop won't boot after Tuesday's update,
check-in desk> tells a reader what the ticket is about before they open it.

Avoid generic subjects like C<error>, C<problem>, or C<help>. They fit every ticket
and none, and they are almost impossible to pick out of a long list or a search.

If you find yourself packing structured detail into the subject, such as an order
number, a device ID, or a category, some of that may be better recorded in a
L<custom field|docs/glossary.pod>, a field your administrator can add to capture data
beyond RT's built-in fields. This matters most where some people reach RT only by
email and never see the full form.

=head2 Set priority, dates, and other fields

The create form has more than the basics. You do not have to fill these in, but they
help RT and your team sort and schedule the work.

=over 4

=item *

B<Priority.> How important the ticket is, so busy queues can surface the urgent
tickets first. A typical RT shows priority as Low, Medium, or High.

=item *

B<Dates: Starts and Due.> B<Starts> is when work should begin; B<Due> is when it
should be finished. RT can sort by due date and highlight overdue tickets, so setting
B<Due> helps the right tickets get attention in time.

=item *

B<SLA.> If your queue uses service level agreements, you may see an B<SLA> field. An
SLA sets response and resolution time targets, and RT can calculate the B<Due> date
from it for you. See the SLA entry in L<Ticket metadata|docs/ticket_metadata.pod>.

=back

=head2 Other ways to create a ticket

=over 4

=item *

B<By email.> Send a message to a queue's email address, and RT creates a ticket from
it. The sender becomes the Requestor, the message subject becomes the ticket subject,
and the message body becomes the first message. Ask your team lead for the address of
the queue you want to reach.

=item *

B<From the homepage.> The B<Quick ticket creation> box on the homepage creates a
ticket without opening the full form. Fill in the subject, queue, and message, adjust
the owner or requestors if you need to, and click B<Create>. Use it when a request
needs only the basics; use the full form from the main menu when you need every field.

=item *

B<From a person's user summary page.> When you already know who a ticket is for, you
can start it from their record. Search for the user, open their user summary page, and
use its B<Quick ticket creation> option. Because you are creating the ticket from that
person's context, RT fills them in as the Requestor for you.

=item *

B<Automatically, from another system.> Tickets do not have to be created by hand. Your
administrator can build simplified, custom web forms for creating tickets, so people
submit specific kinds of requests through a form tailored to them. And because RT has
an API, other systems in your organization, such as a monitoring tool or an online
order system, can create tickets in RT automatically when something happens. You do
not need to be a developer to make use of either: if a routine request could arrive
through a form or straight from another system, ask your administrator what is
possible.

=back

If you don't see one of these options, your RT may not have it enabled; ask your team
lead.

=head2 Next steps

Once the ticket exists, you work it from its display page. Take ownership, reply to
the Requestor, add internal comments, and resolve the ticket when the request is
handled. The L<Getting started with RT|docs/user/getting_started.pod> tutorial covers
this full path from a new ticket to a resolved one, and
L<How to reply to or comment on a ticket|docs/user/reply_vs_comment.pod> covers
replying and commenting in depth.

=head2 For administrators

The create form is configurable. Which fields appear on it, and in what order, is set
by L<Page Layouts|docs/customizing/page_layouts.pod>. Service level agreements are
configured in L<Service Level Agreements|docs/customizing/sla.pod>. Custom ticket-creation
forms are provided by the C<RT::Extension::FormTools> extension.
