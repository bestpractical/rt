=head1 RT Glossary

This document is a glossary of terms used in RT.

=head2 General terms

=over 4

=item Article

Reusable, pre-written text stored in RT, such as a canned answer or a
piece of standard wording, that staff can insert into a ticket reply. Articles help a
team answer consistently. Each article belongs to a class.

=item Asset

A record of something your organization owns or manages, for example a
laptop, a software license, or a piece of equipment. Assets are tracked in RT and can
be linked to the tickets about them. Each asset belongs to a catalog.

=item Catalog

A container that groups related assets, the way a queue groups
tickets.

=item Class

A container that groups related articles, the way a queue groups
tickets.

=item Comment

An internal note added to a ticket. In a default RT, a comment is not
emailed to the ticket's Requestors or Ccs; AdminCcs do receive it. Administrators can
change who is notified. Compare Reply.

=item Custom Field

A field an administrator adds to capture data beyond RT's
built-in fields. RT supports multiple types of custom fields, like
custom dates (e.g., "Approved Date"), dropdowns (e.g., "Issue Type"),
text fields (e.g., "Rejection Details"), and many others. Custom fields can be
defined for most objects in RT including tickets, assets, groups, and users.

=item Lifecycle

The set of statuses a ticket can have and the steps allowed between
them. Each queue uses a lifecycle; RT ships with a default one, and administrators
can define others.

=item Link

A record of the relationship between one object and another in RT.
Links are available on multiple types of objects, most commonly on tickets.
Tickets can link to other tickets, assets, other objects, or internet URLs.
RT's link types include depends on, refers to, and parent/child.

=item Owner

The user responsible for working a ticket. A ticket has exactly one
Owner. Owner is one of a ticket's roles.

=item Portlet

A box on an RT page: each panel on the homepage (a ticket list, the
quick ticket-creation form) and each box on a ticket's display page (The Basics,
People, Dates) is a portlet. Also called a widget.

=item Privileged user

A user with access to RT's full interface, typically a staff
member who works tickets. Privileged users are members of RT's built-in Privileged
group. Compare Unprivileged user.

=item Queue

A container that groups related tickets, like a shared inbox. Many
organizations give each team its own queue, but a queue can be organized around
anything: a product, a project, a stage of work.

=item Reply

A message on a ticket meant for the people the ticket is for. In a
default RT, a reply is emailed to the ticket's Requestors and Ccs. Administrators can
change who is notified. Also called correspondence. Compare Comment.

=item Requestor

The person who initiated the ticket, or on whose behalf it was
created; the person the ticket is being worked for. A ticket can have more than one
Requestor. Requestor is one of a ticket's roles.

=item Resolve

To mark a ticket done. In RT's default lifecycle this sets the
ticket's status to C<resolved>; a customized lifecycle may use different statuses.

=item Right

A permission that controls what a user can see or do in RT, for example
viewing a ticket, replying to it, or creating tickets in a queue. Rights are granted
to users through the groups and roles they belong to, and can apply across all of RT
or only within a single queue, catalog, or class. Administrators grant rights.

=item Role

A named connection between people and a ticket: Owner, Requestor, Cc,
and AdminCc are the roles every ticket has, and administrators can add custom roles.
Roles determine who receives email about the ticket. See
L<Ticket metadata|docs/ticket_metadata.pod>.

=item Scrip

An automated action that may run based on a ticket or asset event. Scrips run on every
update to a ticket or asset. They first evaluate a condition, and if the condition
is true, RT runs the action. RT uses scrips to send most of its notifications.

=item Self Service

RT's simplified interface for unprivileged users. It shows a user
their own tickets and lets them create new ones, without the full staff interface.

=item SLA

A service level agreement: response and resolution time targets assigned to
a ticket. When a ticket has an SLA, RT can automatically calculate its Due
date and track whether those targets are being met. See
L<Ticket metadata|docs/ticket_metadata.pod>.

=item Status

Where a ticket stands in its lifecycle, for example C<new>, C<open>,
or C<resolved> in the default lifecycle. See
L<Ticket metadata|docs/ticket_metadata.pod>.

=item Ticket

RT's record of a single request or task, from arrival to completion.

=item Transaction

One recorded change on a ticket: a reply, a comment, a status
change, and so on. A ticket's history is its list of transactions.

=item Unprivileged user

A user without access to RT's full staff interface, typically a
requester who submits and follows their own tickets. Unprivileged users are members
of RT's built-in Unprivileged group and normally work through the simpler Self
Service interface. Compare Privileged user.

=item Watcher

Someone who receives email about a ticket because of their role on
it. Requestor, Cc, and AdminCc are a ticket's watcher roles: in a default RT,
Requestors and Ccs are emailed replies, and AdminCcs are emailed replies and
comments both. A queue can also have Cc and AdminCc watchers, who watch every
ticket in that queue. Compare Role.

=back
